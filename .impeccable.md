## Design Context

### Users
Correction Notebook is for students and parents working through math mistakes together, primarily on an iPad or web surface. The product should help them capture paper mistakes, verify OCR text, understand the root cause, practice related questions, track mastery, and generate separate student and answer handouts. Students need the next action to be obvious and low-friction; parents need confidence that the notebook is organized, accurate, and useful for follow-up review.

### Brand Personality
The brand should feel calm, trustworthy, and rigorous. The interface should reduce the shame or anxiety around wrong answers while still treating learning records seriously. Copy should be direct and practical: explain what happened, what to do next, and what has changed, without gamified exaggeration or overly technical AI language.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use a warm, paper-like light theme as the default. The current direction is an editorial study desk: warm canvas and panel colors, ink used sparingly for small anchors rather than large heavy blocks, terracotta primary actions, restrained teal status accents, modest 8px radii, and refined Chinese serif display typography for major headings. Prefer durable study-material cues over generic SaaS blue, neon gradients, glass effects, or playful mascot-driven visuals. Dark mode is not required unless explicitly requested later.

### Design Principles
1. Put the next learning action first: capture, review, practice, or print should be visible without explanation.
2. Make mistakes feel manageable: use calm language, clear hierarchy, and supportive status feedback.
3. Preserve trust: OCR, AI analysis, answer keys, and mastery states must look inspectable and editable rather than magical.
4. Design for iPad-first touch use: keep controls large, spacing stable, and layouts readable in both desktop web and compact widths.
5. Keep visual changes disciplined: extend the warm editorial system already in the app instead of introducing unrelated colors, rounded-card clutter, or speculative decorative elements.
